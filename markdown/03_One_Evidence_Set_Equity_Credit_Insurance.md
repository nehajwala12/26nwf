# CreditSwan | One set of evidence for equity, credit and insurance
## Onelife Fitness at 26North

**26North master demo · Markdown edition · 15 October 2026 · Prepared for Andrew Lindquist, operating partner**

> **Illustrative model.** Every Onelife operating, financial and debt figure in this document is an illustrative model (baseline assumptions A-02, A-09), not company data. **Onelife's real acquisition debt is undisclosed**: Kirkland's debt-finance credit on the October 2024 deal implies financing, but lenders and size are not public. The $40m insurance holding is a scenario, not a position. Whether a 26North insurer or lender may hold debt of a 26North portfolio company is an **affiliate-transaction question for counsel and the regulator**; this demo shows the evidence, not an allocation. Public facts are tagged [Public]. Amounts are USD; TTM to September 2026 unless stated.

**The decision:** connect Onelife's systems once, build one source-traced fact base of 20 facts, and let the Alpha Creation Team, the lenders (third-party and, if counsel allows, 26North's direct lending team) and 26North Re / Independent Life read their own view from it. On 15 October that single fact base caught a **$1.2m** misclassified pre-opening add-back and told all three teams the same thing in the same hour, before the compliance certificate was signed. Pilot Onelife for eight weeks, then extend to Composition Brands, where the carve-out makes a single fact base worth the most.

## 02 | Why three teams keep three numbers today

**Three teams pull the same ledger, apply three definitions on three calendars, and then spend days explaining why the answers differ.** None of them is wrong. Each is right for a different contract: the operating plan, the credit agreement and the insurance balance sheet.

| Team | Number they quote | Definition source | Refresh | Typical lag after month end |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Alpha Creation Team / deal team | $79.8m run-rate | Investment committee model | Monthly | 10 to 15 days |
| Lenders / credit monitoring | $75.0m covenant | Credit agreement "Consolidated EBITDA" | Quarterly certificate | Up to 45 days |
| Insurance investments / rating file | $111.8m EBITDAR | Rating methodology, lease-adjusted | Quarterly mark; annual rating refresh | 30 to 90 days |
| Management accounts | $71.4m reported | GL | Monthly | 10 days |

| Cost of keeping three numbers (illustrative, per company) | Hours per quarter today | With one fact base |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equity: rebuild monthly KPI pack and run-rate bridge | 60 | 15 |
| Credit: recompute certificate, refresh model, chase support | 45 | 12 |
| Insurance: refresh rating inputs, mark memo, LP look-through | 35 | 10 |
| Cross-team reconciliation calls when numbers differ | 20 | 3 |
| **Total** | **160** | **40** |

| Gap | What it looks like | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Definition gap | $71.4m vs $75.0m vs $79.8m vs $111.8m quoted in the same week | Every meeting starts with a reconciliation |
| Timing gap | Equity sees September in October; insurance marks on June inputs into November | A problem visible to one team is invisible to another for weeks |
| Evidence gap | Add-backs accepted from the borrower's schedule, not the GL | Errors surface at audit or at a downgrade, not at source |
| Memory gap | Each team keeps its own workpapers | No single answer to "where did this number come from?" |

**The insight:** the four EBITDAs are not a disagreement; they are one fact base read through four contracts. The problem is that nobody holds the fact base, so every disagreement looks like an error.

**Assumptions on this tab:** hours are illustrative estimates for a mid-sized portfolio company with one credit facility and one insurance holding; lags are typical market practice, not 26North's own calendar.

## 04 | Four EBITDA definitions reconciled

**Reported, covenant, run-rate and lease-adjusted EBITDA are one ladder, and every rung names the fact it adds.**

| Rung | Definition | Formula over facts | Before | After 15 Oct |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| Reported EBITDA | Management accounts | F-06 | $71.4m | $71.4m |
| + Pre-opening costs | Permitted add-back, capped at 5% of EBITDA; clubs open 12 months or less only | F-07 | +$3.6m | **+$2.4m** |
| **Covenant EBITDA** | Credit agreement "Consolidated EBITDA" | F-06 + F-07 | **$75.0m** | **$73.8m** |
| + 2026 clubs at run rate | Owner view | F-10 | +$2.6m | +$2.6m |

| + Legacy price increase annualised | Owner view | F-10 | +$0.8m | +$0.8m |
| **Run-rate EBITDA** | Alpha Creation Team, investment committee | Covenant + F-10 | **$79.8m** | **$78.6m** |
| **Lease-adjusted EBITDAR** | Rating file, insurance balance sheet | Covenant + F-08 ($36.8m) | **$111.8m** | **$110.6m** |

| Test | Before | After | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Pre-opening add-back within 5% cap | $3.6m vs $3.75m cap | $2.4m vs $3.69m cap | Cap passes both times; the error was eligibility, not size |

**The insight:** the cap test passed, which is exactly why the error survived. A $3.6m add-back under a $3.75m cap looks clean on a borrower schedule. Only joining the GL cost centres to club opening dates (F-07 × F-05) shows that a third of it belongs to clubs past the 12-month window.

## 11 | Governance, permissions and information barriers

**One fact base does not mean one audience: the facts are shared, but each team sees only what its role, its contracts and the walls allow.** 26North's private equity, credit and insurance businesses are all private-side, but they are not interchangeable, and the conflicts are real.

| Who | May see | May not see | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha Creation Team, PE deal team | All facts, equity view, weekly board, levers, exit values | Credit team's internal credit memos on other borrowers; insurer's allocation decisions | Owner rights; credit and insurance decisions must be independent |
| Third-party lenders | What the credit agreement entitles them to: package, certificate, add-back support | Owner's value plan, exit values, board commentary | Contractual information rights only; nothing flows from CreditSwan directly |
| 26North direct lending (if a lender, subject to counsel) | Lender-entitled information, as a lender | Owner's plan, sponsor deliberations | Lender to an affiliate's company must be walled from the owner side |
| 26North Re / Independent Life investment team | Rating inputs, mark inputs, lender-level information | Owner's plan, live deal deliberations | Insurer duty to policyholders; affiliate-transaction rules |
| Valuation agent, auditor, regulator | Fact versions, traces, definitions | Commentary not relevant to their test | Independence |
| Onelife management | Their own facts and exceptions | 26North's views and commentary | Company is the source, not a recipient of investor views |

| Control | How it works |
|---|---|
| Fact layer shared, view layer walled | Facts carry evidence; views carry opinions. Walls sit on views and commentary |
| Lender information flows through the borrower | CreditSwan flags to Onelife; Onelife's CFO issues to lenders. No back channel |
| Affiliate test before any allocation | A 26North insurer or lender holding Onelife debt is referred to counsel first: insurance holding company affiliate-transaction rules, Bermuda requirements, fund conflict policies, LP disclosures |
| Independent marks | Marks on any affiliate-sourced holding set or validated by a third-party valuation agent re-performing from the trace |
| Related-party history | Josh Harris's family office (HRS) co-owned Onelife with Delos before 26North bought it [Public]; any valuation or allocation file should record this |
| Logging | Every view, export and alert logged by user; quarterly access review by compliance |

**The insight:** the wall gets stronger, not weaker. Today walls are policed by people choosing not to forward spreadsheets. With one fact base, the wall is a permission on a view, logged and reviewable, and the shared layer holds only facts every party could see in the ordinary course.

**Assumptions on this tab:** permissions are a proposed design; whether 26North's direct lending team or insurers may hold Onelife debt, and on what terms, is for counsel and the relevant regulators. Nothing here is legal advice.
