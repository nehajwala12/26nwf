## Onelife Fitness | More members kept, more value per member, and capital sent where it earns the most

**26North master demo · Markdown edition · 15 October 2026 · Alpha Creation Team, Andrew Lindquist (operating partner) and Onelife's CEO, CFO and COO**

> **Illustrative model on a real company.** Onelife Fitness (US Fitness Holdings, McLean VA) and 26North Partners are real. Every Onelife operating, member, financial and debt figure in this document is an **illustrative model** (baseline assumptions A-02 and A-09), consistent with the 26N evidence baseline and the Onelife packet V2; none of it is company data. Public facts carry a source and date. S01–S12 are simulated record references, not connected systems. The $1 join offer is a demo scenario: Onelife's public club pages actually offer free passes and a free Premier trial. No pilots, outreach, integrations or monitoring have been launched. Dollar amounts are USD; "m" is millions; the baseline is an illustrative TTM to September 2026.

**The operating decision:** fix the clubs Onelife already owns before committing more capital to ground-up builds. Seven levers bridge illustrative covenant EBITDA from **$75.0m to about $92.2m** (+$17.2m net, worth roughly **$140–210m of exit value at 8–12x**) without opening a single new club. The sharpest finding: a new build costs **$10–24m** and earns a year-3 cash return of about **12–15%**; a conversion of an existing box costs about **$4m** and earns about **35%**. New builds return about a third as much per dollar.

## 07 | Digital acquisition, free passes and the $1 join offer

**Cheap joins are expensive when they leave within 90 days. The $1 offer filled the NC and AL funnels and emptied them again.**

**Demo scenario:** in W37 the $1 joining-fee offer was extended in NC and AL with no end date. In reality, Onelife's public club pages (fetched 15 Oct 2026) show a free pass or free Premier trial, and the Premier join fee is listed at $5; no $1-join offer was visible. The scenario is built to show how CreditSwan catches a promotion that outlives its purpose.

| Join channel (illustrative, NC/AL clubs, W37–W41) | Joins | 90-day cancel | Cost per retained member |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Full joining fee | 410 | 7.5% | $64 |
| Free pass converted | 520 | 9.8% | $71 |
| $1 join offer | 1,150 | 21.0% | $118 |
| **Total** | **2,080** | **15.5%** | **$94** |

**Digital surfaces:** onelifefitness.com drew ~568k visits, +5.9% MoM, with organic search 35.7% of desktop traffic (Similarweb, Sep 2026). Tests: sessions to booked tours, a fixed AI-answer prompt panel per metro, paid media against matched-metro holdouts, and free pass to join by club.

**Campaign arithmetic:** 1,150 $1-joins × 21% early cancel = 242 lost in 90 days. Against full-fee joiners at 7.5%, that is 155 excess cancels and ~$98k of foregone joining fees at an average $85 fee. The offer only pays if it brings in members who would not otherwise have joined and who stay.

**Assumptions on this tab:** the $1 offer and all channel figures are a demo scenario. Similarweb figures are third-party modelled. Average $85 fee is illustrative (public Basic join fee $79, Premier $5, onelifefitness.com, 15 Oct 2026).

## 08 | Join journey and the first 90 days

**The member decides whether to stay in the first month, so the first month is where the money is.**

| Stage, per 1,000 qualified leads (illustrative) | Count | Leak to investigate |
|---|---:|---|
| Lead (web form, call, walk-in, free pass) | 1,000 | Lead quality by channel |
| Tour or first visit | 620 | Slow response, no tour slot at peak |
| Join | 410 | Price clarity, annual-fee disclosure |
| 4+ visits in first 30 days | 270 | No onboarding session, crowded floor |
| Still billing at day 90 | 362 | 48 cancels = 11.7% of joins |

**Deep dive:** at the five NC/AL clubs, joins on the $1 offer rarely booked the free onboarding session. Members who complete it visit more and stay longer. The fix combines an onboarding booking at the point of join, a day-10 coach call and a day-21 class invitation, not more advertising.

**Unit of success:** members billing at day 90 per 100 joins, with cost per retained member as the guardrail. A tactic that lifts joins but lowers day-90 survival fails.

**Annual-fee moment:** Trustpilot reviewers cite an unexpected annual fee (Trustpilot, 18 reviews, fetched 15 Oct 2026). Disclosing it clearly at join may cost a few joins and save many cancellations and complaints.

**Assumptions on this tab:** funnel counts are a demonstration sample, not a forecast. Onboarding effect is a hypothesis tested in P04.

## 10 | Labour to actual members

**Young clubs are staffed for the members they were supposed to have, not the members who actually come in.**

**Club labour $92.6m (31.5% of revenue):** front desk $24.0m, PT commissions $19.5m, floor and coaches $16.0m, group instructors $9.8m, housekeeping $9.2m, Kids Club $7.6m, club management $6.5m.

| Labour lens | Legacy clubs | 2025 cohort |
|---|---:|---:|
| Labour % of revenue | 29.6% | 47% |
| Members vs ramp plan | n/a | 82% |
| Staff hours vs plan | n/a | 100% |
| Labour $ per visit | $2.70 | $4.90 |

**Mechanism:** schedule front desk, floor and Kids Club hours to the half-hourly check-in curve. Keep instructors and PT as they are, because they drive retention and ancillary. Moving labour from 31.5% to 30.8% of revenue is **$2.0m** net.

**Overlap rule:** labour savings at the seven 2025 clubs are counted here, not again in the ramp lever (Tab 20 deducts $0.4m). Released hours can serve more members or cut paid cost, never both.

**Public context:** about 5,000 team members (company releases, 2026); Indeed 3.3/5 from 402 reviews (fetched 15 Oct 2026); median exercise-trainer pay $22.67 an hour (BLS OEWS, May 2025).
